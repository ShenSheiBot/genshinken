## Discourse map

- Opening/origin: a chat-group impulse to share personal experience about anime criticism.
- Problem statement: mockery of theory-heavy criticism is driven mainly by distrust of theory and misreading of discursive power.
- Section I, exclusion: anime/subcultural history, incorporation by the culture industry, consumer society, privatized everyday experience, and rejection of metadiscourse.
- Section I, discursive power: no fixed authority exists, yet every critic creates a local center of discourse by interpreting and speaking; denying this misunderstands the function of discourse.
- Section II-A, breadth: introduces Simmel's “stranger” as an internal-yet-distanced group member; otaku likewise remain within mainstream society while producing culture through distance and friction.
- Breadth consequences: neutrality, absolute neutrality, and fixed reader effects are impossible; criticism should therefore remain open to residual categories and unusual angles.
- Section II-B, depth: critiques grand theories for missing otaku empathy and reducing multiple causes to capital/consumption alone.
- Dialectical tension: otaku know their attachments are mediated/fictive yet desire real-world efficacy and recognition; culture industry implodes the subculture/mainstream boundary, prompting “hardcore” retreat and fragmented identity.
- Proposal: replace the “trash audience / trash production” loop with “structural thinking” that reads works relationally and reflexively, linking formal analysis to the critic's own subcultural/mainstream position.
- Ending: proposes “otaku self-awareness,” offers schematic breadth/depth diagrams as provisional ideal types, and ends by preferring tolerance over policing.
