## Discourse map

- Introduction: contrasts character analysis by personal life history and by narrative function/database, proposing `relationship` as a third analytic object that links virtual situations to lived social forms.
- Relational method: folds diachronic history into spatial/formal positions, invoking Simmel's “social synthesis” while insisting that relationships retain emotion and human logic.
- Triads: Simmel's three-person relation is presented as both the beginning and endpoint of social analysis, unlike the private romantic illusion of the dyad.
- Love-triangle model: in male-oriented heterosexual works, the core protagonist anchors desire between two female characters while deferring choice and producing factional conflict; the ending converts the triangle into `couple – society/world`.
- Male protagonist types: avoidant/self-castrating versus indiscriminately caring/relationship-enjoying, illustrated through Hachiman Hikigaya and Haruki Kitahara.
- Female positions in male-oriented works: psychologically near `girl-next-door type` versus distant `mysterious type`; family background and social marginality reproduce this distance.
- Female-oriented inversion: the heroine need not accept the same castration; male desire-objects can likewise be organized by near/far distance, with `Daytime Shooting Star` and `Short Cake Cake` as examples.
- Love-triangle dissolution: exclusivity, jealousy, disguise/compensation, and the traumatic departure of the “losing” subject; the most shocking reversal would be the core protagonist's withdrawal enabling same-sex desire between the other two.
- Transition: once the triangle dissolves into `couple – world`, some works begin rather than end. This becomes the general `Byakuyakō` / fugitive-lovers model.
- Byakuyakō model: the third position is society, politics, atmosphere, the world, or another symbolic order rather than a concrete third person; marginal couples gain aesthetic sublimity through conflict with the Big Other.
- Limits of the model: Japanese ACG often abstracts or weakens political mediation, making the triad look like a dyad; Romeo Tanaka's work is discussed as a case where bullying/social reality disrupts the pure fantasy.
- Conclusion: relation-form analysis supplements biography and structural-function analysis; the special love-triangle form collapses into the general couple–society field, whose depoliticizing tendency requires further analysis.
