## Source verification and routing

The Roof essay is an original Chinese work. Its argument routes directly from that source, while quoted titles and Japanese-source quotations are handled separately.

Verified reference points:

- Seikaisha / Sai-zen-sen 15th-anniversary page: https://sai-zen-sen.jp/sa/karanokyoukai/ — preserves the early English subtitle as `……Is nothing id, nothing cosmos`, confirming that the Roof source's `noting` is a transcription error.
- Kodansha's 20th-anniversary edition page: https://www.kodansha.co.jp/book/products/0000053234 — confirms the publication title 『空の境界 the Garden of sinners』 and the work's publication history.
- LUNA SEA official biography: https://sp.lunaseamobile.jp/feature/biography — confirms `LUNA SEA Concert Tour '93-'94 The Garden of Sinners`.
- ABC's official film-series page: https://www.asahi.co.jp/smp/anime/karano/chapter4.html — confirms Japanese forms and readings for 両儀式, 黒桐幹也, the dual personalities 式／織, and the film/chapter framing.

The verified early subtitle is restored in both target editions. This is a bounded correction of a source transcription error, not a silent rewrite of the essay's argument. By contrast, the essay's interpretive claims about hamartia, Augustine, Buddhism, Heidegger, Gnosticism, and the Nasuverse are translated as authored claims rather than fact-corrected into a new essay.

The Japanese target faces a separate provenance problem: many direct quotations in the Roof source are Chinese translations of Japanese novels, glossary entries, game/anime material, or interviews. Bounded verification does not establish the exact Japanese wording for every passage. The Japanese edition therefore carries one scoped relay notice before the first such quotation and does not pretend the subsequent passages are recovered originals.

## Discourse map

- Opening frame: interpret the relation between the Japanese title 『空の境界』 and English subtitle *the Garden of sinners* by splitting the titles into sinner, 空, 境界, and garden.
- Sinner: moves from Greek *hamartia*, the New Testament, Augustine, Kierkegaard, and free will toward the work's “abnormal” figures, whose wrongdoing also marks an inability to reach the emptiness/blankness implied by the title.
- 空: reads *kara* through hollowness, 伽藍, Buddhist emptiness, existential void, and a contrasting peaceful ordinary state represented by Mikiya and 観布子の母.
- 境界 / garden: treats boundaries as barriers separating common sense from mystery and ordinary from abnormal; the same boundary is also an individual's inner gap and a “domain.”
- Shiki versus Araya: the central opposition is cast as Shiki descending from `「 」` toward humanity while Araya climbs from humanity toward `「 」`.
- Recurrent oppositions: yin/yang, 式/織, dual bodies and dual personalities, violence and killing, meaning and meaninglessness, Araya and Alaya.
- Divine descent: the ending reframes the story through human/god relations and Gnostic descent; the prison/garden disappears as ordinary life and common sense return.
- Final synthesis: *the Garden of sinners* is the stage on which abnormal people act before moral recognition and reckoning; 『空の境界』 emphasizes that “abnormality” exists only relative to “common sense” and that the dividing boundary is itself empty.
