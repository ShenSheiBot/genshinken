## Discourse map

- Opening challenge: criticism should not reduce *Persona* to a checklist of Jungian definitions; interpretation should recover awe, artistic intensity, and the work's deeper historical and literary structures.
- Picaresque genealogy: Hashino's “picaresque” framing leads from *Lazarillo de Tormes*, *Guzmán de Alfarache*, and the limping-devil tradition to the social outsider who surveys a corrupt city from below and above.
- Transformation of the type: the Phantom Thieves share exclusion and displacement with picaresque protagonists but reject the genre's cynical upward mobility; they punish powerful wrongdoers and return to ordinary life.
- Persona beyond Jung: Pessoa, pseudonymity, reflection, multiplicity, Lupin, and the modern metropolis broaden “persona” into a problem of identity and self-fashioning rather than a single Jungian keyword.
- Social rebellion: NPC speech, corrupted adults, the false god, and collective passivity shift the enemy from a single tyrant to social resignation, distorted desire, and the wish to surrender agency.
- Heroic form and limit: Campbell's departure-initiation-return pattern still structures the game, but the quiet ending refuses a clean rebirth of society; the Phantom Thieves become rumor while ordinary life continues.
- Tragedy versus striving: Marlowe's Faustus and Tamburlaine, Aristotle's flawed tragic figure, and Goethe's Faust help the author distinguish destructive overreaching from the youthful courage the game ultimately wants to rekindle.
- Ending function: the acknowledgements turn the essay's ethic back on criticism itself—seek what one wants to do, accept future refutation, and participate in a collective culture of more demanding writing.

## Voice card

- Genre and register: ambitious fan criticism with literary history, philosophy, game analysis, and a deliberately high rhetorical pitch.
- Rhythm: long, accumulative sentences and dense name clusters are punctuated by brief questions, aphoristic transitions, image captions, and long quotations.
- Authorial stance: self-deprecating about authority but polemical about shallow interpretation; frequently moves from “I” to exhortative “we.”
- Humor: parenthetical jokes, otaku self-mockery, and colloquial NPC examples should remain visible amid the elevated prose.
- English treatment: literary-critical but not archaic; official franchise vocabulary is preferred where it does not overwrite the source's historical wording.
- Japanese treatment: contemporary である critical prose, preserving the essay's intensity without forcing Chinese clause order; verified Japanese interview wording may be recovered selectively, while unresolved wording becomes naturally attributed summary or indirect discourse.
