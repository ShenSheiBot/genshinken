## Discourse map

- Objective: ask Furukawa Tomohiro to identify an early anime experience that shaped his own directing sensibility, with *Saint Seiya: Legend of Crimson Youth* as the first case.
- Movement: childhood discovery of *Saint Seiya*; later recognition that the two films he remembered most were both directed by Yamauchi Shigeyasu; close reading of the film's silent opening and formal stylization; taboo eroticism in Abel/Saori; the motif of “taking away” Saori and reducing the Saints to ordinary boys; fetishistic bodily violence and stylized still-image techniques.
- Major turns: exuberant formal analysis gives way to taboo/incest as a deliberate directorial strategy, then returns to a healthy boys' recovery narrative and finally to the tension between lavish theatrical animation and TV-derived still-frame technique.
- Ending function: Furukawa identifies constraint-generated stylization as something difficult to reproduce in contemporary anime production, closing the interview on the value of form rather than nostalgia alone.
