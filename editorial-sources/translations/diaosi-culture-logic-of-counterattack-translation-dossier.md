## Discourse map

- Thesis: the self-designation `吊丝` is not merely a confession of low status; through cynicism, self-objectification, and the fantasy of future “counterattack,” it creates a privileged position in which failure is normal and success becomes miraculous.
- Sequence: comparison with Ah Q; definition of the cynical/humorous diaosi; objectification of others and self; the “objective” cynical gaze; asymmetry of imagination between diaosi and `高富帅`; counterattack as the culture's secret; temporal inversion of Ah Q; present paralysis produced by anticipated future satisfaction; genital/castration wordplay; extension into contemporary performances of weakness.
- Major turns and qualifications: the essay distinguishes diaosi from the more direct Ah Q; shifts from objectifying women to self-objectification; then turns the diaosi's claimed objectivity into ironic distance; finally reframes apparent weakness itself as a form of victory.
- Pronoun dependencies: singular `吊丝` frequently shifts into plural `他们`; targets preserve that movement rather than regularizing all references into one number.
- Recurring wording: `吊丝`, `高富帅`, `逆袭`, `物化`, `客观`, `意淫`, `快感`, victory/failure, present/future.
- Ending function: argues that the word `吊丝` may have faded while its temporal logic has diffused into a new social “air.”

## Voice card

- Genre and register: informal theoretical/cultural criticism with internet slang, psychoanalytic vocabulary, deliberate vulgarity, and first-person argumentative asides.
- Sentence rhythm: long causal chains and conceptual restatements alternate with blunt one-line claims, rhetorical questions, and the two-item list.
- First-person and reader address: the author repeatedly uses `我`, `我们`, and imperatives such as `别忘了`; targets retain this direct argumentative pressure.
- Technical density: moderate; terms such as cynicism, objectification, ironic distance, objectivity, castration, and organization of pleasure coexist with Tieba slang.
- Irony / polemic: dismissive phrases (`憨`, `恶心`, `太强了`) and sexual slang are part of the argument and are not sanitized into neutral sociological prose.
- Target treatment: English uses `diaosi` as the stable loanword, with semantic translations for compositional slang; Japanese retains `吊丝` as the foreign culture-specific keyword while translating surrounding slang enough for the argument to function.

## Self-review evidence

### English bilingual fidelity

- Compared every natural paragraph, all five image nodes, all four italic caption paragraphs, the two numbered claims, all numbers, and all quoted slang against the Chinese canonical. Structural audit returned 0 failures and 0 warnings.
- High-risk censorship: `生O器` is never silently expanded to the uncensored Chinese word. English uses the visible mask `genit*ls`; in Markdown the asterisk is escaped (`genit\*ls`) so the public page renders the literal censor mark rather than accidentally creating emphasis markup.
- High-risk vulgarity: `不给日就0分` remains explicitly coarse as `“if she won’t let me fuck her, zero points”`; it was not softened into a generic low-rating remark.
- High-risk comparative logic: `我祖上比你阔` is `my ancestors were richer than you`, not `richer than yours`, so the later reversal `不是过去比你阔，而是未来比你阔` keeps the same person-to-person temporal comparison.
- High-risk conceptual turn: earlier `物化` remains `objectify`; later `客观化` is rendered through `turns himself into an object of observation` and `objective gaze`, avoiding the non-idiomatic draft `objectivizes` while preserving the move to a purportedly detached viewing position.

### English source-free fluency

- Repeated italics on nearly every occurrence of `diaosi` were removed after the first defining use; reason: a long essay set almost entirely in italics at each keyword reads as typographic noise in English and falsely overmarks a term the essay treats as ordinary slang.
- Before: `their families have been diaosi for generations back`; after: `they come from generations upon generations of diaosi`. Reason: the first version was grammatical but calqued Chinese word order.
- The Li Yi Bar paragraph was tightened from `was founded on precisely this kind of cynicism` to `rests on precisely this kind of cynicism`, which carries `建立在……基础上` without making a historical founding claim.
- The counterattack payoff `成为一种加倍的高富帅` was revised from the stiff `becoming a doubled tall-rich-handsome` to `becoming tall-rich-handsome twice over`.
- The future-satisfaction paragraph now reads `the diaosi’s imagined future ... leaves them satisfied with what lies ahead`, avoiding the repetitive `future ... satisfied with the future` while preserving the source's present/future mechanism.
- The final cute-slang line was revised from the unidiomatic `I’m the only trash one` to `I’m the only trash here`.
- The deliberately blunt image caption `纯粹找点图放在这` remains equally throwaway (`Just putting some pictures here for the sake of it.`) rather than being upgraded into a formal editorial caption.

### Japanese bilingual fidelity

- The author name in the first alt text was initially normalized to Japanese-style `実在狗子`; revised back to exact `实在狗子` because it is a contributor identity, not prose to localize.
- `高富帅` and `白富美` were initially left unexplained; first occurrences now carry concise lexical renderings (`高身長・金持ち・イケメン`, `色白・金持ち・美女`) so the compositional slang is actually translated without adding a separate process note.
