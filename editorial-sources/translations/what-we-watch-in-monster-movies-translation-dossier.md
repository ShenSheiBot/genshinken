## Discourse map

- Historical opening: monster cinema from *King Kong* through *Godzilla*, the 1998 U.S. film, *Godzilla: Final Wars*, and *Pacific Rim*.
- Spectacle question: why viewers stay in their seats; destruction and VFX make monster films look like superhero spectacle.
- Counterclaim: *Godzilla* (1954) and *Shin Godzilla* show that plot and institutional/human drama can carry a monster film.
- Main thesis: a good monster film is not a film “about monsters” but a film “about people”; the monster is a conscious natural disaster and also a victim of human intervention.
- Closing of main body: memorable cinema depends on thought rather than effects alone; *Godzilla*'s nuclear/weapon reflection and Serizawa example.
- Fenglinhuoshan note: expands the humanist thesis to tokusatsu, science fiction, anime, *King Kong*, Ultraman, and Kamen Rider; effects are a means, not the theme.
- Li Paopao note: first argues monster cinema can reduce anthropocentrism by confronting humans with nonhuman power and ecological blowback; then argues it can reinforce anthropocentrism by unifying humanity against an irrationalized natural Other.
- Li Paopao conclusion: *Ultraman Gaia* introduces an ecocentric corrective and ultimately imagines reconciliation between human and nonhuman agency.
- Editor note: abrupt, humorous recommendation of *Kamen Rider W*.

## Voice cards

Main Roof voice: informal collective/fan criticism with jokes, profanity-masking (`真他X的`), parenthetical memes, rhetorical questions, and deliberately colloquial dismissals alongside a sincere humanist thesis. Do not elevate it into formal film theory.

## Source discrepancies and translation decisions

- `怪兽篇` in 风林火山's note is contextually an apparent typo for `怪兽片`; the targets render the intended “monster films” while the Chinese canonical remains unchanged.
- `《哥斯拉》极其系列作品` is contextually an apparent typo for `及其系列作品`; targets render “Godzilla and its related series/works” rather than reproducing a nonsensical typo. This is recorded here rather than silently altering the source.
- The main body attributes the 1954 *Godzilla* script to Takeo Murata, Ishirō Honda, and Eiji Tsuburaya. The translation preserves the Roof claim and does not fact-correct the historical canonical.
