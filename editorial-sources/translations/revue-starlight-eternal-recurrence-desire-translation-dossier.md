## Discourse map

- Epigraph and opening question: whether one would accept an eternally recurring world to retain happiness.
- Nana's loop: replaying the 99th Seisho Festival as an attempt to arrest time.
- Nietzschean frame I: Apollonian stasis, Platonism, impotence before “what was,” and the early nihilistic version of eternal recurrence.
- Nietzschean frame II / Deleuzian turn: recurrence as affirmation, becoming, simulacra, and difference rather than reproduction of an original model.
- Nana's two motives: preserving the highest present value and avoiding unpredictable future fate; after defeat, she accepts chance and difference.
- Dionysian / ritual reading of the Seisho Festival and the `Starlight` performance; close visual reading of props, vessels, statue, tower, and timings.
- Desire frame I: Platonic lack and psychoanalytic impossibility.
- Desire frame II: Deleuzian productive desire, desiring-machines, social machines, and the campus/festival environment as part of the object of desire.
- Ending: Nana shifts from lack to overflowing gift, from goddess of despair to goddess of hope, and the essay closes with Nietzsche's printed English poem.
