# English source destinations — October 10, 2026

This is an editorial research snapshot, not a continuously maintained availability
monitor. Publication identities were checked against original websites, publisher
and journal records, institutional repositories, book contents, and archived
original pages. Recheck a destination when its URL, edition, or source attribution
changes, or when a reader reports a broken link. HTTP access alone does not establish
publication identity; an access challenge does not establish that a work is absent.

## Result

The archive contains 660 source units: 431 posts and 229 book chapters. Their English
routes now account for:

This pass adds 419 disposition entries: 252 external English sources and 167
cross-language exclusions. It does not delete or rewrite any article. Previously
prepared Chinese-source translations and Japanese dispositions are unchanged.
The 167 exclusions comprise 165 Japanese, one Russian, and one Spanish source.
An exclusion is a publishing decision, not a claim that no English edition exists.

Reader-facing entries are in `source/_translations/language-dispositions.json`.
Each external entry names the work and its creator, publication, and destination.
Where a Chinese post is an excerpt, the coverage note distinguishes the selection
from the complete linked work. Book and subscription-journal links are not described
as freely accessible full text.

## Books, anthologies, and serial installments

The feminist-theory selections are matched to the numbered readings in *Reading
Feminist Theory: From Modernity to Postmodernity*, edited by Susan Archer Mann and
Ashly Suzanne Patterson, Oxford University Press, ISBN 9780199364985. The linked
[university bookstore listing](https://miamioh.ecampus.com/reading-feminist-theory-from-modernity/bk/9780199364985)
provides the complete contents and access to the volume. These links identify the
anthology selections; they do not claim that every complete original essay is
reproduced in the Chinese post. Reading numbers were checked against titles, not
inferred from incidental references to other readings in the body.

Book chapters retain their own routes even where several point to the same volume.
*Confronting Capital and Empire* entries identify individual chapter titles and
authors. *Phenomenology: The Basics* and Gary Kemp's philosophy-of-language chapter
point to the relevant editions, rather than silently substituting a newer edition.
Split installments of a single article identify their shared original, including
both parts of the *To Be Heroine* interview. The final *Sound! Euphonium 2* entry
links both episode 12 and episode 13 articles.

## Archived originals and the conference manuscript

Three original websites have archive destinations: Hiroki Azuma's “Superflat
Japanese Postmodernity,” Mark Langer's “The End of Animation History,” and FAR's
*Yama no Susume* episode analysis. These are captured original pages, not Chinese
retranslations or unrelated substitutes.

Nick Admussen's “The Postpandemic, the Postsocialist, and Jile Disike (Disco Elysium)”
is positively identified in Cornell's
[May 4, 2024 workshop program](https://events.cornell.edu/event/pandemic_archives_media_geopolitics_and_temporalities_of_crisis_day2).
The local Chinese introduction describes an expanded manuscript supplied with the
author's permission for translation. The gateway therefore identifies the paper
and links the organizer's program **as a program**, not as a downloadable article.
No public full-text destination was located in this search. This is the one new
English-source route that supplies a conference record rather than the work or a
publication through which it can be obtained. It must not be counted as recovered
full text.

The co-editor also describes the special issue as work in progress in a
[UCI interview](https://humanities.uci.edu/news/interview-comparative-literature-alum-shiqi-lin).
The author’s [Cornell publication page](https://as.cornell.edu/people/nick-admussen)
does not supply a download of this manuscript. These findings support its identity
and provenance, not a claim that an English manuscript does not exist.
