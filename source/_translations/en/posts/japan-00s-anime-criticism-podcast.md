**Zuanshi:** In recording this episode, I came to understand how postmodern theory and the postmodern condition differ, and how they are connected. The “post-” in “postmodern” signifies both temporal succession and a theoretical response. As theory, the postmodern may be no more than an account of many philosophical problems offered by particular thinkers; as an era, however, it has undoubtedly already enveloped us all.

**Yeshen Renjing:** The postmodern is not modernity’s polar opposite; rather, it is multiple temporalities still layered together within an age regarded as already distinct from the modern. In a sense, grand narratives have not vanished: broken down and crushed into fragments, they have been absorbed into the many small narratives that compete and coexist. We could say that ACGN works—anime, comics, games, and novels—offer us one way to grasp these small narratives. Erciyuan’s connections with the postmodern are not limited to consumer society. Perhaps “paradigm shifts” in scholarship—Hiroki Azuma’s game-like realism in relation to the Japanese cultural context, for instance, or Thomas Lamarre’s *The Anime Machine* in relation to film theory—can bring something fresh to our understanding of animation and individual works.

**Tiankeng:** What matters more than these theories and thinkers bundled together under “the postmodern” is the context in which the thinkers put forward their views and theories, and the problems they were addressing. If we want to discern the features of contemporary society through erciyuan—or, to put it another way, through the consumption of popular culture today—we still need more locally grounded and more concrete analysis and practice, building on the work of those who came before us.
