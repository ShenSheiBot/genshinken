What makes this anthology distinctive:

*From the anthology’s introduction, translated here from the Chinese excerpt. The bracketed notes are those of the Chinese translator.*

> A central principle of this anthology is that the pursuit of social justice is feminist theory’s reason for being; understanding the political implications of different feminist perspectives is therefore imperative. This emphasis on politics distinguishes the anthology from others that simply arrange feminist readings by topic. We are concerned that when feminist writings are merely set alongside one another, readers find it harder to distinguish their underlying political assumptions, their strategies for social change, and their target groups. This anthology, by contrast, emphasizes the relationship between feminist theory and political practice so that these important distinctions do not become blurred.
>
> Second, the anthology pays particular attention to differences and diversity among feminist voices, visions, and perspectives, which vary with race, class, gender, and global location. Although many other feminist anthologies attend to these important differences among women, this anthology connects these differences in social location to political concerns. A core principle of the anthology is our belief that the particular problems people face in everyday life often reflect their differing standpoints or social locations. Its historical approach will also make clearer how these problems and political concerns have changed over time.
>
> Third, this anthology calls for a broader account of what is usually recognized as theoretical activity, encompassing multiple sites of theory-making both inside and outside the academy. It celebrates not only the value of knowledge of social life, or knowledge gained through everyday living, but also forms of theorizing other than Western forms of abstract logic (Collins, 1990; King, 1990). Alongside more traditional forms of theorizing, the anthology includes biographies, poetry, short stories, magazines, song lyrics, and even animation. In making the case for multiple sites of theorizing, we recognize that “doing theory” extends far beyond classrooms, conference papers, or academic journals, and that alternative forms of theorizing [Chinese translator’s note: Presumably this refers to the biographies, poetry, and so on mentioned above] also represent a “distillation of experience” that can serve the cause of women’s liberation (Lorde, 1984: 38).
>
> …

> The sixth distinctive feature of this book is that its organization allows readers to see paradigm shifts in feminist thought more clearly. A paradigm shift is a fundamental change in the theoretical assumptions underlying an entire way of thinking or worldview. We argue that such a shift took place in U.S. feminism between the 1970s and the 1990s, when modern feminist thought was challenged by feminist perspectives turning toward the postmodern. By comparing and contrasting these two modes of social thought, readers will see how their sharply different views of social life, social thought, and political practice are central to some of the major conflicts and debates within feminism today.
>
> Seventh, the anthology devotes three entire chapters and more than thirty readings to colonialism, imperialism, and globalization. Here readers will learn how feminists from the first through the third waves have addressed these subjects, and how feminist thinking about them has changed over time. Because the place we now call the United States of America has been both a colony and a colonizer [Chinese translator’s note: This means that the American empire was first a victim that broke free of its status as a British overseas colony, then became a perpetrator itself by colonizing other regions and peoples], readers will gain a better understanding of how this turbulent history has affected women of different races, classes, sexual orientations, and nationalities.
>
> Finally, many chapters include readings that present feminist critiques of other feminist theoretical perspectives. Uma Narayan’s essay in Chapter One, for instance, shows how a non-Western postcolonial theorist responds to various Western approaches to feminist epistemology. In Chapter Three, an essay by Suzanna shows how a radical feminist critiques queer theory. This enables readers to see not only the strengths of different perspectives but also their weaknesses. The overall aim is to help readers develop the critical analytical skills needed to understand and analyze new social circumstances. These skills help us question conventional wisdom about the countless features of social life that are often taken for granted.
