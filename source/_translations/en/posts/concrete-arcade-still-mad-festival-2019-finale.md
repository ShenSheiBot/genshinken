## 1. Introduction

Lab on Roof × ZERO MAD TEAM.

Through this festival, Lab on Roof and ZERO MAD TEAM hope to provide a welcoming forum for still-image MAD—fan edits made by animating and compositing still images—and opportunities for creators to collaborate and discuss their work, while also offering viewers a distinctly Lab on Roof approach to appreciating the form.

## 2. Closing Address

Working closely with ZERO MAD TEAM, and with the support of independent judges including Zhizui (支醉), Luci (鸬鹚) and Fengyuejiang (风月酱), Lab on Roof has now completed the judging for this Still-Image MAD Festival.

Honestly, this was our first time organizing anything like this. With no relevant experience, I was pretty anxious, and yes, we did keep people waiting and run into setbacks. But thanks to the kindness and help of the creators and everyone involved, I think, as the organizer, that the event as a whole had quite a lot going for it~

Some of you may be wondering why Lab on Roof organized this event. The answer is really quite simple: our interest in and love for still-image MAD as a moving-image form, and for its possibilities. As for everything else we feel, I think I’d rather let the works in this festival express it than put it into words. It’s all there in them—

### [【Still-Image】I Want to Eat Your Pancreas](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1MJ411h7Fn)

- At 0:45, I’d suggest moving right first, then left. There’s text, after all: moving from right to left while the text has to be read from left to right creates a bit of a conflict.

**Luci:**

**Lab on Roof:**

- The black, white and red palette feels really good with the instrumental music. Around the vocal pause at 0:30–0:35, I feel the transitions into and out of it, and the fit between sound and image, could be better~ In terms of emotional expression, the visual narrative reveals Sakura’s fate midway through, then moves into “my” remembrance and farewell. Bringing back the diary, *Living with Dying*, as a narrative thread in the later section would give it more resonance~

### [【2020 MAD New Year Festival Individual Entry/Still-Image】Arknights \[War · Purge\]](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1b7411v7hv)

*Runtime: 03:07; figures shown on the page: 5,162 views, 10 danmaku.*

**Zhizui:**

- You could lengthen the transition at 0:18 with Motion Tile or something similar. Don’t just switch between the two scenes: generally, you want to add one to five completely blurred intermediate images that don’t belong to either scene. The transition’s duration needs to match the music.
- Be bolder with the lighting; don’t be afraid of overdoing it. Add more of that divine light streaming down through the clouds, that Tyndall-effect sort of thing. Each shot lingers a little long at the moment, so richer content would help. But by “stronger,” I don’t mean quantity—I mean intensity, in brightness, size and so on.
- The rain at 1:20 is too timid as well. I had to look closely for two seconds before I could tell there really was rain.
- For the blinks at 1:40 and 2:37, unless there’s a particular expressive purpose, there’s no need to blink twice in such a short time. If you insist on it, give the character a different expression before and after—more of a rueful smile afterward, for example—instead of just blinking for the sake of blinking.
- There aren’t quite enough elements built up in the final scene. You could make it more epic.

**Fengyuejiang:**

- The compositing is quite good, and so is the design, but you need to study transitions.

**Luci:**

- The visuals are clean, but the staging stays too close to the source material to carry the music. Not enough rise and fall.

**Lab on Roof:**

- Within individual shots, I can feel the camera movement directing the eye toward the subject of the narrative. Several of the hand close-ups are very good. I think using a wider range of shot sizes in combination would make it even better. Try more variety in the transitions and changes in frame proportions next time, too~

### [【Still-Image MAD】The Emperor’s Academy](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1q7411h71j)

*Runtime: 01:12; figures shown on the page: 1,805 views, 1 danmaku.*

**Zhizui:**

- Give the font choices a bit more thought.
- Big blocks of text are hard to read. Some still-image MADs use huge blocks deliberately made illegible, as visual elements. Here, though, you actually want people to read the text. The wording needs work.
- The backgrounds are a bit monotonous. Avoid conspicuous edges, especially the kind at 0:07.
- Remember to align things with the edge. At 0:11, for example, there’s a white border beside the character on the left.
- You’ve still got some learning to do with text composition, kid. A lot of the text flashes away before my eyes can even move over and make it out.
- It’s best not to stop the camera movement. Keep things moving.
- The text flying past at 0:15 doesn’t show who’s speaking. Some dialogue needs to be composed together with the character; otherwise people will generally read it as narration.
- The light at 0:31 isn’t plausible. That light spot couldn’t be elliptical like that. Don’t just stretch it by scaling. I won’t even get started on the 3D.
- Overall, the backgrounds are rather monotonous, and it isn’t very fluid either. There’s a lot of room to improve. Also, remember not to make the eyes blink so slowly. You could film yourself and see how many seconds a blink takes.
- At 0:48, I suspect you took a shortcut. It’s not unheard of to reuse one large image, but just moving sideways across it looks cheap. Blur the surroundings, add flickering, add glow, rotate as you push in or pull back—whatever you do, it can’t be so plain.
- At 0:50, the ripples are going over the shoes. Try stepping in water yourself and see whether the ripples go over your shoes.
- At 0:53, don’t go straight to a white screen. You could have a huge sword flash sweep across the screen before it goes white.
- You could save that final GIF for later, as a way to gauge your technical progress—come back and make it in six months or a year, for example.

**Fengyuejiang:**

- The overall level of finish is low, and the motion curves are rather stiff. Spend more time looking at how the original video does it.

**Luci:**

- Quite bold, but you still need to work on the visual execution.

**Lab on Roof:**

- The editing pace feels somewhat out of balance both within the visual narrative and in its relationship to the music. You could try adjusting the frame proportions and shot size within a single shot to invest the subject of the narrative with the feeling you want to express, rather than rushing to cut or moving too quickly.

### [【Still-Image MAD/Made in Abyss】To the Far Side of the Abyss](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1D7411N7mv)

*Runtime: 01:33; figures shown on the page: 317,000 views, 214 danmaku.*

**Zhizui:**

- The hair at 0:40 could use some more work. It’s moving too fast; you could lower the frequency and increase the range.
- At 0:26, the descent could continue instead of having the character stop moving. You could also add a little side-to-side sway.
- For the melting tree at 0:32, I’d suggest using a color key to isolate some orange-yellow areas, blurring them, then adding glow and overexposure. As it stands, it’s not very clear that the tree has been melted through. Did you leave the floating spark particles’ blending mode unchanged?
- At 0:35, Ozen’s left side—her back—could be darker. The stronger the contrast, the better. The shadow could enter from outside the frame, and move faster. The light and shadow cast on the wall need changes in brightness.
- At 0:50, never mind the hammer having no reflections: in a shot this long, it ought to block the sunlight, making the light flicker a few times during the push-in. The grass is too uniform in color, with no clear distinction between near and far. You could work on the tonal values here, or use blur. You could also build the grass in layers; slight color differences would make it even better. On first viewing, it took me a second to realize that was grass. It isn’t recognizable enough. If you feel there’s nothing else you can do, add a 180-degree rotation to the pullback at the start of the shot. Even that would be better than such a long shot with nothing happening.
- The abyss at the end is stunning, though it might be better if a couple of characters in some of those lines of text were red. Two other shots I like are the descent at 0:25 and the scene at 0:58.

**Fengyuejiang:**

- Adding some background to the opening text could actually help it blend better with the style of what follows.

**Luci:**

- Everything is in place. The arrangement could be more ingenious.

**Lab on Roof:**

- The sound–image coordination is very accomplished, though I do slightly suspect that the visual narrative is being pulled along by the song’s rhythm. The music coming in a little early at 1:16 is a small flaw. I really like the handling of light and dark, and the light sources, at 0:28. The shot at 0:45–0:50 stays still a little too long; I feel you could push in a little. Really satisfying overall.
